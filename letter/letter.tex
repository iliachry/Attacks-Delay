\begin{abstract}
Transport-layer automatic repeat request (ARQ) protocols ensure reliable packet delivery over lossy channels but trigger severe positive feedback loops in adversarial settings. In this letter, we formulate a unified stochastic framework modeling end-to-end packet sojourn times and stability boundaries across multi-node networks subject to active destruction and modification attacks. By coupling renewal-reward theory with fixed-point traffic conservation and hypoexponential moment matching, we reveal a fundamental capacity divergence: pre-service destruction attacks preserve server processing capacity, whereas post-service modification attacks waste complete service intervals, precipitating premature queue saturation. In multi-hop chains, end-to-end timeouts induce super-linear delay explosion and geometric shrinkage of the stable operating envelope. High-fidelity discrete-event simulations confirm our analytical predictions across all topologies with relative errors under 1\%.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Adversarial queueing, ARQ feedback loops, renewal-reward process, hypoexponential distribution, network stability.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{ecent} deployments of autonomous swarms, tactical ad-hoc networks, and mission-critical IoT systems operate over hostile wireless channels subject to active jamming, payload corruption, and packet dropping \cite{wood2002denial, pelechrinis2011denial}. Adversarial packet destruction and removal dynamics relate conceptually to the foundational theory of G-networks with negative customers pioneered by Gelenbe \cite{gelenbe1991product, gelenbe1993gnetworks}. To ensure delivery across unreliable links, transport protocols employ timeout-governed automatic repeat request (ARQ) retransmissions \cite{fall1996simulation}.

While retransmissions restore reliability, they create an insidious \textit{positive feedback loop}: adversarial packet drops or corruption induce timeouts, which generate retransmissions, elevating the aggregate network load $\Lambda^*$ and escalating queueing delay, in turn triggering cascades of further timeouts. This feedback manifests as abrupt ``cliff-edge'' saturation boundaries.

Prior work on adversarial networks largely assumes static routing or asymptotic fluid limits \cite{garetto2008modeling}. In this letter, we bridge stochastic queueing theory \cite{kleinrock1975queueing, ross1996stochastic} and adversarial threat modeling. We develop exact and semi-analytical closed-form models for:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Destruction vs. Modification Duality:} Demonstrating that post-service corruption exhausts server resources substantially faster than pre-service destruction.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Hop Hypoexponential Journeys:} Deriving Gamma moment-matching formulations \cite{altiok1985approximate, cox1955use} for $N$-node feedforward chains with stage-wise traffic attenuation.
    \item \textbf{Feedback Mesh Invariance:} Establishing Erlang visit summations for $N$-node symmetric meshed architectures.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Universal Theoretical Framework}
Consider external Poisson arrivals entering a network at rate $\lambda$. Each packet attempts transmission until success. Let $P_{\text{succ}}$ be the probability that an attempt completes without adversarial interference and within timeout window $W$. The number of attempts $A$ follows a geometric distribution with $E[A] = 1/P_{\text{succ}}$.

By renewal-reward theory \cite{ross1996stochastic}, the total end-to-end sojourn time $E[D]$ is:
\begin{equation}
E[D] = (E[A] - 1) E[D_{\text{fail}}] + E[D_{\text{succ}}] + (E[A] - 1) B
\label{eq:renewal}
\end{equation}
where $E[D_{\text{fail}}]$ and $E[D_{\text{succ}}]$ are the expected durations of failed and successful attempts, and $B \ge 0$ is a deterministic backoff. By traffic conservation, the total arrival rate $\Lambda^*$ satisfies the non-linear fixed-point equation:
\begin{equation}
\Lambda^* = \frac{\lambda}{P_{\text{succ}}(\Lambda^*)}
\label{eq:fixed_point}
\end{equation}
The network remains stable if and only if $\Lambda^* < \Lambda_{\text{crit}}$, where $\Lambda_{\text{crit}}$ is the bottleneck service rate.

\section{Single-Node Attack Dynamics}
Let a single queue operate with service rate $\mu$, timeout $T$, and attack probability $p$.

\subsection{Pre-Service Destruction (Case 1)}
In destruction attacks, packets are dropped before receiving service. The effective rate occupying the server is $\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \Lambda^*(1-p)$. Server utilization is $\rho = \lambda_{\text{eff}}/\mu < 1$. The total time in system satisfies $P(W_q + S \le T) = 1 - \rho e^{-(\mu - \lambda_{\text{eff}})T}$.
\begin{align}
P_{\text{succ}} &= (1 - p)\left[1 - \frac{\Lambda^*(1-p)}{\mu} e^{-(\mu - \Lambda^*(1-p))T}\right] \\
E[D] &= \frac{1}{P_{\text{succ}}}\left[\frac{\Lambda^*(1-p)}{\mu(\mu - \Lambda^*(1-p))} + \frac{1-p}{\mu}\right] \nonumber \\
&\quad + \left(\frac{1}{P_{\text{succ}}} - 1\right)B
\end{align}
\textbf{Capacity Bound:} Stability requires $\Lambda^*(1-p) < \mu \iff \lambda < \mu$. Destroyed packets do not consume service capacity.

\subsection{Post-Service Modification (Case 2)}
In modification attacks, corrupted packets are detected only \textit{after} full service ($S \sim \text{Exp}(\mu)$). Thus, all attempts consume server capacity ($\rho = \Lambda^*/\mu$). The sojourn time is exponential with rate $\mu - \Lambda^*$:
\begin{align}
P_{\text{succ}} &= (1 - p)\left(1 - e^{-(\mu - \Lambda^*)T}\right) \\
E[D] &= \frac{1}{(1-p)(1 - e^{-(\mu - \Lambda^*)T})(\mu - \Lambda^*)}
\end{align}
\textbf{Capacity Bound:} Stability requires $\Lambda^* < \mu \iff \lambda < \mu(1-p)(1 - e^{-\mu T})$. Corrupted packets waste full service cycles, reducing capacity by a factor of $(1-p)$. Fig. \ref{fig:single} demonstrates this fundamental divergence.

\section{Multi-Hop Feedforward Networks}
Consider an $N$-node tandem chain ($0 \to 1 \to \dots \to N-1$). Because packets attacked at stage $i$ drop out, traffic attenuates along the chain: $\Lambda_i^* = \Lambda_0^*(1-p)^i$.

\subsection{Gamma Moment-Matching for Hypoexponential Delay}
By Burke's theorem \cite{burke1956output}, stage delays $X_i \sim \text{Exp}(\mu - \Lambda_i^*)$ have mean $T_i = (\mu - \Lambda_i^*)^{-1}$ and variance $T_i^2$. The total journey $S_N = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} X_i$ is hypoexponential with mean $\mu_S = \sum T_i$ and variance $\sigma_S^2 = \sum T_i^2$. We approximate $S_N \sim \text{Gamma}(k, \theta)$ with shape $k = \mu_S^2/\sigma_S^2$ and scale $\theta = \sigma_S^2/\mu_S$:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{no\_to}} = P(S_N \le W) = \frac{\gamma(k, W/\theta)}{\Gamma(k)}, \quad P_{\text{succ}} = (1 - p)^N p_{\text{no\_to}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact Delay Decomposition}
Conditioned on success ($S_N \le W$), the truncated Gamma expectation is $E[D_{\text{succ}}] = \mu_S \frac{\gamma(k+1, W/\theta)}{\gamma(k, W/\theta)}$. Failed attempts average:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
E[D_{\text{fail}}] &= \frac{1}{1 - P_{\text{succ}}} \Bigg[\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} (1-p)^i p \sum_{j=0}^i T_j \\
&\quad + (1-p)^N (1 - p_{\text{no\_to}}) E[S_N \mid S_N > W]\Bigg]
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $E[S_N \mid S_N > W] = \mu_S \frac{1 - \gamma(k+1, W/\theta)/\Gamma(k+1)}{1 - \gamma(k, W/\theta)/\Gamma(k)}$.

The total delay $E[D] = (P_{\text{succ}}^{-1} - 1)E[D_{\text{fail}}] + E[D_{\text{succ}}]$ exhibits super-linear growth with $N$ (Fig. \ref{fig:feedforward}a). As $N$ increases from 2 to 4, the stability region contracts catastrophically (Fig. \ref{fig:feedforward}b).

\section{Symmetric Feedback Meshes}
In an $N$-node symmetric feedback mesh, packets transition uniformly between nodes with probability $\frac{N}{N+1}$ or exit with probability $\frac{1}{N+1}$. By Jackson symmetry, each node has clearance rate $\gamma = \mu - \Lambda^*$, and $k$ node visits follow an Erlang-$k$ distribution $S_k \sim \text{Erlang}(k, \gamma)$ with CDF $F_k(W) = 1 - \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \frac{(\gamma W)^j e^{-\gamma W}}{j!}$.

With reach weights $u_k = (1 - p)^{k-1} (\frac{N}{N+1})^{k-1} F_{k-1}(W)$:
\begin{align}
E[V] &= \sum_{k=1}^\infty u_k, \quad P_{\text{succ}} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty (1 - p)^k \frac{N^{k-1}}{(N+1)^k} F_k(W) \\
\Lambda^* &= \lambda \frac{E[V](\Lambda^*)}{P_{\text{succ}}(\Lambda^*)}
\end{align}
Unlike tandem chains where Node 0 bottlenecks the system, feedback meshes distribute retransmissions evenly, yielding graceful delay scaling (Fig. \ref{fig:feedback}).

\section{Experimental Validation and Conclusion}
We validated our analytical models against high-fidelity discrete-event simulations in SimPy \cite{matloff2008introduction} with 80–200 replications and 20,000 time units per run. As summarized in Table \ref{tab:val}, relative errors are consistently below 1\% across multi-node topologies.

In conclusion, this letter established the first unified analytical framework characterizing the non-linear coupled dynamics between adversarial attacks, retransmissions, and multi-hop network topologies. The results provide definitive criteria for dimensioning timeout timers and network capacity in contested environments.
