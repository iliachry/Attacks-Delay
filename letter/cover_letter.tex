\textbf{Context and Key Scientific Contributions:} \\
Modern tactical ad-hoc, sensor swarm, and IoT networks operate over hostile wireless channels subject to active jamming and packet corruption. While transport protocols employ automatic repeat request (ARQ) retransmissions to ensure reliability, retransmissions inherently induce a non-linear positive feedback loop that precipitates abrupt, catastrophic queue saturation.

In this work, we develop the first unified, closed-form stochastic framework that captures this coupled adversarial-retransmission dynamic across multi-node topologies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
    \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Destruction vs. Modification Duality:} We mathematically prove that post-service modification attacks degrade capacity $(1-p)$ times faster than pre-service destruction attacks by consuming complete server cycles for corrupted packets.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Hop Hypoexponential Moment-Matching:} We derive a two-parameter Gamma moment-matching model for $N$-node feedforward chains under stage-wise attenuation, proving super-linear delay growth and severe contraction of the stable operating envelope.
    \item \textbf{Mesh Invariance:} We establish Erlang multi-visit expansions for symmetric feedback meshes, demonstrating that distributed load-balancing prevents single-node bottlenecking.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Validation:} All theoretical models are verified against SimPy discrete-event simulations across five topological case studies, achieving relative errors consistently below 1\%.
\end{enumerate}
