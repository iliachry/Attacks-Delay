\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a robust theoretical framework for analyzing the average sojourn time and throughput in N-node network architectures subjected to malicious attacks and timeout constraints. We investigate two fundamental topologies: a symmetric feedback network and a tandem feedforward network. Using renewal theory and moment-matching approximations (Gamma distribution), we derive closed-form expressions for end-to-end delay. Our results are validated against extensive SimPy-based discrete-event simulations, showing a discrepancy of less than 1\% in stable operational regions. We specifically highlight the counter-intuitive "attack invariance" property in symmetric feedback systems and the high sensitivity of tandem chains to hop-by-hop failures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As network architectures grow in complexity, the interaction between malicious interference (attacks) and congestion-related timeouts becomes critical for performance estimation. Traditional M/M/1 models often fall short in predicting end-to-end delay when every failure triggers a complete retransmission from the source. In this work, we reconcile principled queueing theory with empirical simulation results for N-node feedforward and feedback networks.

\section{System Model}
We consider a network of $N$ nodes, each providing service with rate $\mu$. External packets arrive at the network according to a Poisson process with rate $\lambda$.

\subsection{Attack and Timeout Model}
At each node, a packet faces a Bernoulli attack trial with probability $p$. If an attack is detected (discovered after service), the packet is dropped and must be retransmitted from its original entry node. Furthermore, a timeout window $W$ is enforced. If the total journey time of an attempt exceeds $W$, the attempt is considered failed, and a retransmission is triggered.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}

\subsection{Feedback Network}
In the feedback network, a packet at node $i$ either exits the system with probability $1/(N+1)$ or transitions to one of the $N$ nodes with uniform probability.

The effective traffic rate $\Lambda^*$ at each node is determined by a fixed-point equation:
\begin{equation}
\Lambda^* = \lambda \frac{E[V]}{P_{succ}(\Lambda^*)}
\end{equation}
where $E[V]$ is the expected number of visits per attempt and $P_{succ}$ is the probability of a successful attempt. We model the journey time of $k$ visits as an Erlang distribution with shape $k$ and rate $\gamma = \mu - \Lambda^*$.

The total sojourn time is given by:
\begin{equation}
E[D] = (E[A]-1)E[D_{fail}] + E[D_{succ}]
\end{equation}
where $E[A] = 1/P_{succ}$ is the expected number of attempts.

\subsection{Feedforward (Tandem) Network}
For the tandem network, the traffic rate at node $i$ depends on the success probability of all preceding nodes $0 \dots i-1$:
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_i = \Lambda_0 (1-p)^i P(S_i \le W)
\end{equation}
Since the sum of independent exponential delays with different rates (due to varying $\Lambda_i$) follows a hypoexponential distribution, we approximate the journey time $S_i$ using a Gamma distribution by matching the first two moments:
\begin{equation}
E[S_i] = \sum_{j=0}^{i} \frac{1}{\mu - \Lambda_j}, \quad Var(S_i) = \sum_{j=0}^{i} \frac{1}{(\mu - \Lambda_j)^2}
\end{equation}
